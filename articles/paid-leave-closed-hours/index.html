With five weeks off, a one-person business is closed 79% of the year

France’s standard five weeks of paid leave, applied to a business with nobody else to answer, pushes its closed hours from 77% to 79% of the year. The calculation and how to handle the gap.

Holidays turn a closed evening into a closed month

79.3%

of the hours in 2026 fall outside opening hours for a 9-to-5 business that also takes five weeks off

French employees earn 2.5 working days of paid leave per month, 30 working days or five weeks a year. Five weeks is 25 weekdays. Taken off 2026’s 252 working days, that leaves 227 days of 8 hours.

1 − (252 − 25) × 8 h ÷ 8,760 h = 1 − 1,816 ÷ 8,760 = 79.3%

Sources: Service-Public.gouv.fr, paid leave (fiche F2258); 2026 calendar and public holidays from Service-Public.fr.

The self-employed have no statutory paid leave; five weeks is used here as a common benchmark. A business with staff can rotate holidays and stay open.

The self-employed have no legal entitlement to paid leave, but most take holidays, and the French benchmark is five weeks. For a business where nobody else can answer, those weeks are weeks without a reply.

The calculation

An employee earns 2.5 working days (jours ouvrables) of leave per month, 30 a year: five weeks. Counted in weekdays, five weeks is 25 days.

The difference looks small over a year, but it is concentrated: two or three weeks in August and one at Christmas, when enquiries do not stop.

Before you go on holiday

  • Say it on your site: show the dates you are away and when you will reply.
  • Update your voicemail and email auto-reply with the same dates.
  • Keep answering the easy questions: a chat agent can reply about your services and prices, and collect contact details for when you are back.
  • Plan your return: block the first morning back for the leads that came in while you were away.
