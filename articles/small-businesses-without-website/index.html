One French small business in three still has no website

65% of French very small and small businesses have a website, and for the first time social media accounts are ahead. What the other 35% miss, and what a website should do for those who have one.

A third of small businesses cannot be found on their own site

35%

of French very small and small businesses (TPE and PME) have no website presenting their business

The 2025 France Num barometer, run for the French ministry of the economy, surveyed 11,021 businesses. 65% have a website presenting the business, stable over a year, while 66% have a social media account: the first time social media comes out ahead.

100% − 65% = 35%

Source: Baromètre France Num 2025 (DGE), 11,021 businesses surveyed from 26 March to 18 April 2025, report of 29 September 2025.

Survey of businesses with fewer than 250 employees. A social media page can play part of a website’s role, but you do not control it the same way.

Every year, the France Num barometer asks French very small and small businesses how they use digital tools. The 2025 edition has a surprise.

What the barometer found

  • 65% have a website presenting the business, unchanged over a year.
  • 66% have a social media account: for the first time, more than have a website.
  • 84% have at least one online visibility tool.
  • 26% use artificial intelligence, twice as many as in 2024, and 14% use a chatbot, assistant or AI search tool.

Why a website still matters

A social media page is rented space: the platform decides who sees it, how it looks and what happens to your contacts. A website is yours. It is also what search engines and AI assistants read when someone asks who can help them.

A website that works for you

  • Say clearly what you do, where and for whom, on the first screen.
  • Show how to reach you and when you answer.
  • Collect enquiries at any hour: a form, or a chat that answers questions and takes contact details.
